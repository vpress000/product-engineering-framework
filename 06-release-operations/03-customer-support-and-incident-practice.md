# Customer Support, Incident Response, and Service Continuity

## Purpose

Define how teams support users and respond when the product behaves unexpectedly. Adapt the formality to service criticality, customer expectations, and regulatory obligations.

## Support model

Document support channels, operating hours, severity definitions, response expectations, escalation routes, service ownership, known limitations, and customer communication responsibilities. Keep support knowledge connected to release notes, runbooks, and known issues.

## Incident response

For a material incident, establish an incident lead, affected service owner, technical responders, communications owner, and decision log. Confirm user impact, contain the issue, protect data, restore service, communicate appropriately, and preserve relevant evidence. Security/privacy incidents require the designated escalation and notification process.

## Runbooks

A runbook should state its purpose, trigger, scope, prerequisites, access requirements, diagnostic checks, safe mitigation steps, recovery/rollback steps, verification, escalation, and last-reviewed date. Never place passwords, tokens, or other secrets in runbooks.

## Post-incident learning

For significant incidents, conduct a blameless review covering timeline, impact, detection, contributing conditions, response effectiveness, root and systemic causes, what worked, and prioritized corrective actions with owners and due dates. Avoid unsupported single-cause conclusions.

## Service continuity

Define service dependencies, critical business functions, recovery priorities, backup/restore expectations, RTO/RPO where relevant, communications, and manual workarounds. Test restoration and recovery procedures periodically, not only backup creation.

## Support-to-product feedback

Classify recurring tickets and user pain, connect them to product outcomes or defects, and feed validated patterns into discovery and prioritization. Protect personal information in support exports and AI-assisted analysis.
