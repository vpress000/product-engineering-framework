# Operating Principles

## Product value first

Prioritize validated user and business outcomes, not feature volume. State the problem, target users, expected outcome, and evidence before committing substantial engineering effort.

## Discovery before commitment

Use research, data, prototypes, technical spikes, and stakeholder input to reduce uncertainty. Distinguish evidence from hypotheses. A discovery activity should answer a decision-relevant question.

## Explicit context and decisions

Maintain a concise product profile, decision records, known constraints, assumptions, risks, and open questions. Do not make a future reader infer why an important choice was made.

## Small, coherent increments

Deliver useful increments that can be reviewed, tested, and rolled back or corrected. Group related features when they share capabilities, data models, interfaces, dependencies, or acceptance evidence. Keep groups small enough to integrate and validate safely.

## Quality is everyone's responsibility

Developers, designers, product owners, and QA contribute to quality. Shift validation left, automate stable checks, and keep a proportionate human review for high-impact decisions.

## Secure and privacy-aware by design

Identify data sensitivity, trust boundaries, abuse cases, access controls, and legal obligations early. Collect and retain only the data required for legitimate product purposes.

## Design for operability

A feature is not complete merely because it works in a developer environment. Consider deployment, monitoring, support, failure modes, recovery, documentation, and ownership.

## Preserve before replacing

For existing products, inventory existing artifacts and behaviors. Retain good practices and working assets. Replace only where a defined gap, risk, or measurable benefit justifies the cost.

## Tailor with intent

Apply mandatory controls for critical risks. Tailor other practices to product context. Record meaningful exceptions and revisit them when the context changes.

## Continuous learning

Use incidents, customer feedback, delivery metrics, test outcomes, and retrospectives to improve both the product and the engineering system.
