# Product Strategy and Discovery

## Purpose

Ensure engineering investment is connected to real user needs and measurable product outcomes.

## Product strategy baseline

Maintain a concise product strategy containing:

- Vision and mission.
- Problem/opportunity and why it matters now.
- Target users, buyers, and stakeholders.
- Value proposition and differentiation.
- Strategic themes and desired outcomes.
- Business model and key constraints.
- Product principles and non-goals.
- Success measures and review cadence.

## Discovery loop

1. Identify a user or business problem.
2. Gather evidence from users, analytics, support, market research, or domain experts.
3. State hypotheses and assumptions explicitly.
4. Explore possible solutions, including non-software options.
5. Test the riskiest assumptions with interviews, prototypes, experiments, or technical spikes.
6. Evaluate desirability, feasibility, viability, accessibility, security, and operational impact.
7. Decide to proceed, revise, defer, or stop.
8. Capture evidence and update the roadmap/backlog.

## Outcome-oriented planning

Write outcomes as changes in user or business behavior, not merely delivery of a feature. Pair each outcome with a measure, baseline where available, target or desired direction, and observation period.

## MVP discipline

An MVP is the smallest coherent product or capability that can test a meaningful value hypothesis. It is not simply a large backlog cut in half. Define the intended learning, target cohort, essential journey, minimum quality and safety bar, and what is deliberately excluded.

## Product review

Review strategy when user evidence, market conditions, business priorities, technology constraints, or risk changes materially. Keep a decision history for major pivots.
