# Product Lifecycle, Launch, and End of Life

## Purpose

Connect product definition and engineering delivery to launch, adoption, ongoing customer support, evolution, and responsible retirement. Not every product needs a formal go-to-market program; tailor the depth to its business model and audience.

## Before launch

Define the launch audience and cohort, value proposition, onboarding path, pricing/packaging if applicable, support model, documentation, communications, training, analytics, service capacity, and launch risks. Confirm that customer-facing claims match validated product behavior and known limitations.

## Launch readiness

Coordinate product, engineering, design, operations, support, security, legal/compliance, and marketing responsibilities as applicable. Establish launch owner, go/no-go criteria, communication plan, support escalation, rollback/mitigation, monitoring, and first-review date.

## Adoption and feedback

After launch, monitor agreed product outcomes and critical journeys. Collect feedback from analytics, interviews, support, sales/customer success, and operational signals. Distinguish usage from value: a feature being used does not prove it solves the intended problem.

## Product evolution

Use outcome reviews to decide whether to improve, expand, maintain, consolidate, or retire capabilities. Review usability, accessibility, compatibility, localization, reliability, support burden, security, and cost as the product evolves.

## Deprecation and end of life

For a feature, integration, API, or complete product being retired:

1. State the rationale and decision owner.
2. Identify affected users, customers, partners, contracts, and dependent systems.
3. Assess data export, retention, deletion, archival, and migration obligations.
4. Define notice periods, replacement options, support, and compatibility windows.
5. Plan staged disablement, monitoring, rollback or recovery, and final shutdown.
6. Validate that dependent systems and support processes have transitioned.
7. Preserve necessary audit and decision history, then close access and infrastructure safely.

Do not decommission a capability solely because it appears unused in incomplete telemetry. Validate with product owners and affected stakeholders.
