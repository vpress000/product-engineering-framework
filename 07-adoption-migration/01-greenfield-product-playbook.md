# Greenfield Product Development Playbook

## Objective

Create a product foundation from an idea without overbuilding before the problem and solution are sufficiently understood.

## Stage 1 — Opportunity framing

**Activities:** describe the problem, target users, current alternatives, strategic fit, constraints, and evidence gaps.

**Outputs:** product brief, opportunity statement, initial assumptions, discovery plan.

**Exit:** sponsor agrees the opportunity is worth validating.

## Stage 2 — Discovery and validation

**Activities:** research users, map journeys, test assumptions, assess desirability/feasibility/viability, explore risks, and validate the riskiest unknowns.

**Outputs:** discovery report, personas or user segments where useful, validated needs, evidence, risk summary.

**Exit:** decision to proceed, pivot, defer, or stop.

## Stage 3 — Product definition and MVP

**Activities:** define vision, outcomes, target users, MVP scope, non-goals, success measures, and roadmap hypotheses.

**Outputs:** product strategy, PRD/product brief, prioritized outcome backlog.

**Exit:** product scope and success criteria are approved.

## Stage 4 — Requirements, experience, and architecture baseline

**Activities:** specify key journeys and acceptance criteria, define relevant NFRs, prototype important flows, evaluate architecture and technology options, and identify security/privacy needs.

**Outputs:** requirements baseline, UX artifacts, architecture overview, relevant ADRs, initial test and operations strategy.

**Exit:** the first delivery scope is sufficiently understood and technically feasible.

## Stage 5 — Delivery foundation

**Activities:** establish repository, development workflow, CI, environments, coding standards, automated checks, test data, monitoring baseline, and ownership.

**Outputs:** working development pipeline, environment/run instructions, Definition of Ready/Done.

**Exit:** team can build, validate, and deploy a safe increment.

## Stage 6 — Implementation increments

**Activities:** decompose and group related features, implement small changes continuously, review code, run tests, integrate, and validate collectively at group boundaries.

**Outputs:** accepted implementation groups, test evidence, updated documentation.

**Exit:** MVP scope meets acceptance and quality criteria.

## Stage 7 — Launch readiness and release

**Activities:** validate security, support, operational readiness, data handling, monitoring, rollback, customer communication, and launch criteria.

**Outputs:** release record, release notes, operational handover, known-issues list.

**Exit:** authorized release owner approves launch.

## Stage 8 — Learn and evolve

**Activities:** monitor product outcomes and reliability, collect feedback, review incidents, prioritize improvements, and revisit strategy.

**Outputs:** outcome review, updated roadmap, improvement backlog.

## Greenfield cautions

Do not select architecture based on fashion. Do not build every future capability into the MVP. Do not skip privacy/security because there are few users yet. Do not confuse a prototype with production readiness.
